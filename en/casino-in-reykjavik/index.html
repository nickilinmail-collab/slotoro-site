Quick answer: There are no land-based casinos in Reykjavik or anywhere in Iceland. Gambling is heavily restricted by Icelandic law. However, both locals and visitors can access internationally licensed online casinos.

Why Are There No Casinos in Reykjavik?

Iceland has some of the strictest gambling laws in Europe. The only legal gambling options are the state-run lottery (Happdrætti Íslands) and a limited number of slot machines in licensed venues. Private casinos, both land-based and online, are not licensed domestically.

Attempts to legalize casinos have been made in the Icelandic parliament (Althingi) multiple times, most recently in 2023, but none have passed. The health ministry and public health authorities have consistently opposed casino legalization.

What Gambling Options Exist in Reykjavik?

  • Happdrætti Íslands — the national lottery, available at retail locations throughout Reykjavik
  • Slot machines — limited number in licensed bars and venues (called "spilavinir" in Icelandic)
